# Cable Fly

## Overview
The cable fly is an isolation exercise that targets the pectoralis major using a cable machine. Unlike the dumbbell fly where resistance decreases at the top, the cable machine provides constant tension throughout the entire range of motion — making it arguably the superior fly variation for chest hypertrophy. It is one of the best finishing movements for chest day and is excellent for developing chest definition, the inner chest, and the mind-muscle connection.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Pectoralis Major (mid and inner chest)
- **Secondary:** Anterior Deltoid (front shoulder)
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Biceps (lightly), Rotator Cuff

## Equipment
- Cable machine with two adjustable pulleys
- D-handle attachments (one on each side)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Set both pulleys to the desired height (see variations below)
2. Attach a D-handle to each cable
3. Stand in the center of the cable machine
4. Grab one handle in each hand and take one step forward to create tension
5. Stagger your feet slightly for balance — one foot slightly in front of the other
6. Lean forward slightly from the hips — about 10-15 degrees
7. Start with arms extended out to the sides, palms facing forward or inward
8. Maintain a slight bend in the elbows throughout

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Bring both hands together in a wide arc in front of your chest
3. Squeeze the chest hard as the hands meet at the center
4. Cross one hand over the other slightly at the peak for maximum inner chest contraction
5. Slowly return your arms back out to the sides in a controlled arc
6. Feel the full stretch across the chest at the end position
7. Exhale at the peak contraction and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 10-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-45 secs |
| Mind-muscle connection | 2-3 | 12-15 | 45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- The cable fly is not a strength movement — do not chase heavy weights
- Increase weight by the smallest increment only when you can complete all reps with full range of motion and a strong squeeze
- Focus on the quality of the contraction over the amount of weight — feel every rep

## Key Cues
- **"Hug a tree"** — the most common cue, describes the wide arc motion perfectly
- **"Squeeze at the center"** — actively contract the inner chest as hands come together
- **"Cross at the top"** — crossing hands slightly at the peak maximizes inner chest activation
- **"Never let go of the tension"** — control the return phase, never let the cables pull you back
- **"Soft elbows throughout"** — maintain a slight, fixed elbow bend for the entire set

## Common Mistakes

- **Straightening the elbows** — turns the movement into a press and stresses the elbow joint. Fix: maintain a soft, fixed bend throughout
- **Letting the cables snap back** — loses the eccentric benefit and risks shoulder injury. Fix: control the return phase for 2-3 seconds
- **Standing too upright** — reduces chest activation. Fix: lean forward 10-15 degrees from the hips
- **Using too much weight** — reduces range of motion and removes the stretch. Fix: use a weight where you feel the full stretch on every rep
- **Not crossing at the top** — misses the inner chest contraction. Fix: cross one hand over the other at the peak of every rep

## Cable Fly vs Dumbbell Fly

| | Cable Fly | Dumbbell Fly |
|---|---|---|
| Tension at bottom | High (full stretch) | High |
| Tension at top | High (constant) | Low (gravity removed) |
| Resistance curve | Constant throughout | Decreases at top |
| Inner chest activation | Superior | Good |
| Equipment needed | Cable machine | Dumbbells and bench |
| Best for | Hypertrophy and definition | Stretch and variety |
| Recommended | Yes — primary fly choice | Yes — good alternative |

The cable fly is superior to the dumbbell fly for hypertrophy because the constant tension keeps the chest under load throughout the entire range of motion — including at the top where the dumbbell fly loses resistance.

## Pulley Height Variations

| Pulley Position | Target Area | Description |
|---|---|---|
| High pulley (above head) | Lower chest | Arms travel downward and inward |
| Mid pulley (shoulder height) | Mid chest | Arms travel straight across |
| Low pulley (below waist) | Upper chest | Arms travel upward and inward |

Rotating through all three positions across different sessions gives complete chest development from top to bottom.

## Where It Fits In A Chest Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Push ups                               (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Bench Press                    (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Incline Barbell or Dumbbell Press      (upper chest compound — do this third)
4. Incline Dumbbell Press                 (upper chest hypertrophy — do this fourth)
5. Dumbbell Chest Fly                     (isolation — do this fifth)
6. Cable Fly                              (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

The cable fly is the perfect final exercise on chest day. By this point the chest is fully pumped from compound work and the constant tension of the cable provides a deep, satisfying finish to the session.

## Tempo Recommendation
For maximum hypertrophy use a **2-1-2 tempo**:
- **2 seconds** opening (eccentric) — feel the stretch building
- **1 second** squeeze at the center — maximize the contraction
- **2 seconds** returning (eccentric control)

## Safety Considerations
- Always maintain control of the cables — never let them snap back uncontrolled
- Keep the core braced throughout to protect the lower back from the pulling force of the cables
- If you feel shoulder impingement, adjust the pulley height or reduce the range of motion
- Start light — cable machines feel lighter than they are and the shoulder is in a vulnerable position

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| High to low cable fly | Pulleys above head, pull downward | Lower chest emphasis |
| Low to high cable fly | Pulleys below waist, pull upward | Upper chest emphasis |
| Single arm cable fly | One arm at a time | Greater range of motion, core activation |
| Crossed arm cable fly | Cross arms at every rep | Maximum inner chest activation |
