# Barbell Bench Press

## Overview
The barbell bench press is the king of upper body compound movements. It is the most effective exercise for building overall chest size, strength, and thickness. It simultaneously works the chest, shoulders, and triceps making it the cornerstone of any chest day. It is one of the three powerlifting movements alongside the squat and deadlift.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Pectoralis Major (chest)
- **Secondary:** Anterior Deltoid (front shoulder), Triceps
- **Stabilizers:** Rotator Cuff, Serratus Anterior, Core

## Equipment
- Barbell
- Flat bench
- Power rack or bench press station
- Weight plates
- Spotter (recommended for heavy sets)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Lie flat on the bench with your eyes directly under the barbell
2. Plant your feet flat on the floor — hip width apart
3. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width (roughly 1.5-2x shoulder width)
4. Retract and depress your shoulder blades — squeeze them together and pull them down
5. Create a slight natural arch in your lower back — your glutes and upper back should remain on the bench
6. Unrack the bar by locking out your elbows and moving it over your chest

### The Movement
1. Take a deep breath and brace your core
2. Lower the bar in a slight diagonal path toward your lower chest (nipple line)
3. Keep your elbows at roughly 45-75 degrees from your torso — not flared at 90 degrees
4. Touch the bar lightly to your chest — do not bounce
5. Press the bar back up and slightly back toward the rack in the same diagonal path
6. Lock out at the top and exhale
7. Reset your breath and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-6 | 3-5 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-5 | 6-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Add 2.5kg to the bar each session when you can complete all reps with good form
- If you stall for 2 consecutive sessions, deload by 10% and build back up
- Track every session — weight, sets, and reps without exception

## Key Cues
- **"Bend the bar"** — imagine trying to bend the bar outward with both hands, this externally rotates the shoulders and protects the rotator cuff
- **"Pull the bar to your chest"** — activates the lats and creates a more stable pressing platform
- **"Push yourself into the bench"** — drives leg force through the body and into the press
- **"Elbows at 45-75 degrees"** — the single most important cue for shoulder safety
- **"Chest up"** — reinforces scapular retraction and a strong pressing position

## Common Mistakes

- **Elbows flared at 90 degrees** — the most common mistake, places enormous stress on the shoulder joint. Fix: tuck elbows to 45-75 degrees from the torso
- **Bouncing the bar off the chest** — reduces muscle tension and risks injury. Fix: touch lightly and press immediately under control
- **Lifting the glutes off the bench** — shifts the movement and removes stability. Fix: keep glutes on the bench throughout
- **Uneven grip** — causes bar path deviation and uneven chest development. Fix: use the knurling marks on the bar to ensure even grip width
- **Not retracting the scapula** — removes the stable pressing platform and increases shoulder injury risk. Fix: squeeze shoulder blades together before unracking

## Bar Path
The bar does not travel in a straight vertical line — it moves in a slight diagonal:

```
Start position:    directly over shoulders
Lowering path:     diagonally toward lower chest
Bottom position:   lower chest / nipple line
Pressing path:     diagonally back toward shoulders
End position:      directly over shoulders
```

This diagonal path is more mechanically efficient and safer for the shoulder joint than a straight up-and-down path.

## Grip Width Guide

| Grip Width | Effect |
|---|---|
| Narrow (inside shoulder width) | More tricep emphasis |
| Shoulder width | Balanced chest and tricep activation |
| Slightly wider than shoulder width | More chest emphasis — most common |
| Very wide | Reduces range of motion, increases shoulder stress — not recommended |

## Where It Fits In A Chest Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Light dumbbell flyes or push ups    (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Bench Press                 (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Incline Dumbbell Press              (upper chest compound — do this third)
4. Cable Flye or Pec Deck              (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

The bench press should always be performed first on chest day when your muscles and nervous system are fresh — it is the most demanding and most important chest movement.

## Safety Considerations
- Always use a spotter when pressing near your maximum
- If training alone, use a power rack with safety bars set just below chest height
- Never press to absolute failure without a spotter or safety bars in place
- If you feel sharp pain in your shoulder, stop immediately — do not push through shoulder pain

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 5 minutes light cardio or arm circles
2. 2 x 10 push ups
3. 1 x 10 with empty barbell
4. 1 x 6 at 50% of working weight
5. 1 x 4 at 70% of working weight
6. 1 x 2 at 85% of working weight
7. Begin working sets

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Close grip bench press | Hands inside shoulder width | Tricep emphasis |
| Paused bench press | 1-2 second pause on chest | Builds strength off the chest |
| Tempo bench press | 3-4 second descent | Increases time under tension |
| Board press | Press to boards on chest | Builds lockout strength |

## Related Exercises
- **Incline Barbell/Dumbbell Press** — targets upper chest
- **Dumbbell Bench Press** — greater range of motion, good for hypertrophy
- **Cable Flye / Pec Deck** — isolation movement for chest finishing
- **Push Up** — bodyweight alternative, excellent warm up tool
