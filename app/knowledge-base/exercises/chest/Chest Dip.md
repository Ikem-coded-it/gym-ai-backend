# Chest Dips

## Overview
Chest dips are a bodyweight compound exercise that targets the lower chest, front shoulders, and triceps. They are one of the few exercises that effectively isolate the lower pectoralis major — an area that is difficult to target with flat pressing movements alone. Chest dips are a highly effective mass builder and can be progressively overloaded by adding weight via a dip belt, making them suitable for all experience levels.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Lower Pectoralis Major, Triceps
- **Secondary:** Anterior Deltoid (front shoulder)
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Rhomboids, Serratus Anterior

## Equipment
- Parallel dip bars (slightly wider than shoulder width preferred)
- Dip belt with weight plates (for weighted progression)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Grip the dip bars with both hands and push yourself up to the starting position
2. Lock out your elbows fully at the top
3. Cross your feet behind you for stability
4. This is your starting position

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Lean your torso forward — approximately 30-45 degrees (this is critical for chest activation)
3. Let your elbows flare slightly outward as you descend
4. Lower yourself until your upper arms are parallel to the floor or slightly below
5. Feel the stretch across the lower chest at the bottom
6. Drive yourself back up by squeezing the chest and straightening the elbows
7. Do not lock out completely at the top — stop just short to keep tension on the chest
8. Exhale on the way up and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 4-8 | 2-3 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 8-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Bodyweight endurance | 2-3 | 15-20 | 45-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Begin with bodyweight until you can complete 3 sets of 12 reps with good form
- Add weight using a dip belt — start with 5kg and increase by 2.5kg when you can complete all reps
- Alternatively add reps each session before adding weight

## Key Cues
- **"Lean forward"** — the most important cue for chest dips. An upright torso turns this into a tricep dip. Lean forward to activate the chest
- **"Elbows out"** — letting elbows flare slightly outward shifts load to the chest
- **"Go deep"** — descend until upper arms are at least parallel to the floor for full lower chest activation
- **"Control the descent"** — take 2-3 seconds on the way down for maximum muscle stimulus
- **"Squeeze at the top"** — actively contract the chest as you return to the starting position

## Common Mistakes

- **Staying too upright** — shifts all the load to the triceps and removes chest activation entirely. Fix: lean your torso forward 30-45 degrees throughout the movement
- **Not going deep enough** — reduces lower chest stretch and limits muscle growth. Fix: descend until upper arms are parallel to the floor
- **Locking out at the top** — removes tension from the chest. Fix: stop just short of full lockout on every rep
- **Elbows too close to the body** — reduces chest activation. Fix: allow a slight natural flare of the elbows
- **Rushing the descent** — wastes the eccentric phase. Fix: take 2-3 seconds to lower yourself on every rep

## Chest Dips vs Tricep Dips

| | Chest Dips | Tricep Dips |
|---|---|---|
| Torso angle | Leaning forward 30-45 degrees | Upright — straight up and down |
| Elbow position | Slightly flared outward | Tucked close to the body |
| Primary target | Lower chest | Triceps |
| Depth | Deep — upper arms parallel | Moderate |
| Best for | Chest development | Tricep development |

The difference between a chest dip and a tricep dip is almost entirely in the torso angle and elbow position. The same bars, the same movement — but the lean changes everything.

## Where It Fits In A Chest Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Push ups                               (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Bench Press                    (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Incline Barbell or Dumbbell Press      (upper chest compound — do this third)
4. Incline Dumbbell Press                 (upper chest hypertrophy — do this fourth)
5. Chest Dips                             (lower chest compound — do this fifth)
6. Dumbbell or Cable Fly                  (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

Chest dips work excellently as a lower chest movement after your primary pressing work. At this stage of the workout you can focus on the mind-muscle connection with the lower chest rather than chasing maximum weight.

## Why Chest Dips Are Unique
Chest dips are one of the only exercises that effectively target the lower chest:

```
Upper chest:  Incline press variations
Mid chest:    Flat bench press
Lower chest:  Chest dips — one of very few effective options
```

Neglecting chest dips leaves the lower chest underdeveloped, giving the chest a flat appearance from the side. A well developed lower chest creates the defined, full look that separates a complete chest from an underdeveloped one.

## Safety Considerations
- Never bounce at the bottom — the shoulder joint is under significant stress at the bottom of the dip
- If you feel sharp pain in the front of the shoulder, reduce your range of motion or avoid this exercise temporarily
- Warm up the shoulders thoroughly before weighted dips
- People with existing shoulder injuries should approach this exercise with caution

## Assisted Dips For Beginners
If you cannot complete a full bodyweight dip yet:
- Use an assisted dip machine — it counterbalances a portion of your bodyweight
- Use a resistance band looped over the bars and under your knees
- Build up to full bodyweight before adding external weight

## Weighted Progression

| Level | How |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Bodyweight — focus on form and depth |
| Intermediate | Dip belt with 10-20kg |
| Advanced | Dip belt with 20-40kg+ |

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted dips | Add weight via dip belt | Progressive overload beyond bodyweight |
| Assisted dips | Machine or band assistance | Builds strength for beginners |
| Ring dips | Use gymnastic rings | Greater instability and chest activation |
| Slow eccentric dips | 4-5 second descent | Maximum time under tension |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Bench Press** — primary flat compound chest movement
- **Cable Fly (High to Low)** — another effective lower chest isolation option
- **Decline Bench Press** — barbell alternative for lower chest targeting
- **Push Up** — bodyweight compound warm up movement
