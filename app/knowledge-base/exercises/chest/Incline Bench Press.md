# Incline Bench Press

## Overview
The incline bench press is a compound upper body exercise performed on a bench set at a 30-45 degree angle. It is the most effective exercise for targeting the upper chest (clavicular head of the pectoralis major), an area that is often underdeveloped when training exclusively with the flat bench press. A well developed upper chest gives the chest a full, complete appearance. It is an essential movement in any serious chest training program.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Upper Pectoralis Major (clavicular head)
- **Secondary:** Anterior Deltoid (front shoulder), Triceps
- **Stabilizers:** Rotator Cuff, Serratus Anterior, Core

## Equipment
- Barbell or dumbbells
- Adjustable incline bench
- Power rack or incline bench station
- Weight plates

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Set the bench to a 30-45 degree angle — 30-35 degrees maximizes upper chest activation, steeper angles shift load to the shoulders
2. Lie back on the bench with your eyes directly under the barbell
3. Plant your feet flat on the floor
4. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width
5. Retract and depress your shoulder blades — squeeze them together and pull them down
6. Create a slight natural arch in your lower back
7. Unrack the bar by locking out your elbows and moving it over your upper chest

### The Movement
1. Take a deep breath and brace your core
2. Lower the bar in a controlled path toward your upper chest (just below the collarbone)
3. Keep your elbows at roughly 45-75 degrees from your torso
4. Touch the bar lightly to your upper chest — do not bounce
5. Press the bar back up to the starting position over your upper chest
6. Lock out at the top and exhale
7. Reset your breath and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 4-6 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 8-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Expect to lift 15-20% less weight than your flat bench press — this is completely normal
- Add 2.5kg when you can complete all reps with good form across all sets
- If progress stalls for 2 consecutive sessions, deload by 10% and build back up

## Key Cues
- **"Bar to upper chest"** — the bar should touch just below the collarbone, not the middle chest
- **"Elbows at 45-75 degrees"** — same as flat bench, critical for shoulder safety
- **"Chest up"** — reinforces scapular retraction and keeps the upper chest in the strongest pressing position
- **"Squeeze the chest at the top"** — actively contract the pecs at lockout for maximum muscle activation
- **"Drive feet into the floor"** — creates full body tension and a more powerful press

## Common Mistakes

- **Bench angle too steep (above 45 degrees)** — shifts the primary load from the upper chest to the front deltoids, turning it into a shoulder press. Fix: keep the angle between 30-45 degrees
- **Bar touching the mid or lower chest** — defeats the purpose of the incline angle. Fix: lower the bar to the upper chest just below the collarbone
- **Elbows flared at 90 degrees** — stresses the shoulder joint. Fix: tuck elbows to 45-75 degrees
- **Losing scapular retraction during the set** — destabilizes the shoulder. Fix: actively maintain the shoulder blade squeeze throughout every rep
- **Using flat bench weight expectations** — the incline is a harder movement and requires less weight. Fix: ego aside, use the weight that allows good form

## Bench Angle Guide

| Angle | Primary Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 15-25 degrees | Lower upper chest | Very similar to flat bench |
| 30-35 degrees | Upper chest | Sweet spot for upper chest activation |
| 45 degrees | Upper chest / front delt | Maximum upper chest range |
| Above 45 degrees | Front deltoid | No longer a chest exercise |

## Flat vs Incline Bench Press Comparison

| | Flat Bench Press | Incline Bench Press |
|---|---|---|
| Primary target | Middle chest | Upper chest |
| Weight used | Heavier | 15-20% lighter |
| Shoulder stress | Moderate | Slightly higher |
| Importance | Foundation movement | Essential for complete chest |
| Do both? | Yes | Yes |

## Where It Fits In A Chest Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Push ups or light dumbbell flyes      (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Bench Press                   (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Incline Bench Press                   (upper chest compound — do this third)
4. Cable Flye or Pec Deck                (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

The incline bench press is placed after the flat bench press because the flat bench allows heavier loading and builds overall chest thickness. The incline then targets the upper chest specifically while you still have enough energy for quality sets.

## Safety Considerations
- Always use a spotter when pressing heavy on the incline
- If training alone, set the safety bars on the rack just below your upper chest
- The incline position increases shoulder stress compared to flat bench — never skip the warm up
- If you feel impingement or sharp pain in the front shoulder, reduce the bench angle

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 10 push ups
2. 1 x 10 with empty barbell on incline
3. 1 x 6 at 50% of working weight
4. 1 x 4 at 70% of working weight
5. Begin working sets

## Dumbbell Incline Press Alternative
If barbells are unavailable or you want more range of motion:
- Use dumbbells at the same 30-45 degree angle
- Dumbbells allow a greater stretch at the bottom and more natural wrist rotation
- Slightly lower weight than barbell incline due to the stabilization demand
- Excellent alternative for hypertrophy work

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Incline dumbbell press | Dumbbells at 30-45 degrees | Greater range of motion |
| Close grip incline press | Narrow grip on incline | Upper chest and tricep emphasis |
| Paused incline press | 1-2 second pause on chest | Eliminates bounce, builds strength |
| Tempo incline press | 3-4 second descent | Increases time under tension |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Bench Press** — flat compound movement for overall chest thickness
- **Cable Flye** — isolation movement that provides a deep chest stretch
- **Pec Deck / Machine Flye** — isolation finisher for upper and mid chest
- **Push Up** — bodyweight warm up and activation movement
