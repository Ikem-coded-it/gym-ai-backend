# Front Raise

## Overview
The front raise is an isolation exercise that targets the anterior (front) deltoid. It involves raising the arms directly in front of the body to shoulder height. While the front deltoid already receives significant stimulation from compound pressing movements like the bench press and shoulder press, the front raise provides direct isolation for those who want complete front delt development. It is considered a supplementary movement rather than a primary shoulder exercise and should be placed at the end of shoulder day.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Anterior Deltoid (front delt)
- **Secondary:** Lateral Deltoid (side delt — lightly), Upper Trapezius
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Serratus Anterior, Biceps (lightly)

## Equipment
- Dumbbells (most common)
- Barbell
- Cable machine with low pulley
- Weight plate

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart
2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand in front of your thighs
3. Palms facing down (pronated grip) or facing each other (neutral grip)
4. Maintain a slight bend in the elbows throughout
5. Stand tall with a neutral spine

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Raise one or both arms directly in front of you in a smooth arc
3. Keep the arms straight with just a slight elbow bend
4. Raise until the arm is parallel to the floor — shoulder height
5. Hold for 1 second at the top
6. Lower the arm slowly back to the starting position — take 2-3 seconds
7. Do not swing or use momentum
8. Exhale at the top and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 10-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 2-3 | 12-15 | 30-45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- The front raise is a light isolation movement — do not chase heavy weights
- Increase weight by 1-2kg only when you can complete all reps with strict form
- Front delts are already heavily worked by pressing — they do not need heavy loading here

## Key Cues
- **"Stop at shoulder height"** — going higher shifts load to the traps. Fix: stop when arm is parallel to the floor
- **"Slow on the way down"** — the eccentric phase builds more muscle than the raise itself
- **"No swinging"** — keep the torso completely still throughout every rep
- **"Slight elbow bend"** — never lock out the elbows during the movement
- **"Lead with the knuckles"** — keeps the palms facing down and maximizes front delt activation

## Common Mistakes

- **Using too much weight and swinging** — removes front delt isolation entirely. Fix: use light weight and strict controlled form
- **Raising above shoulder height** — shifts load to the traps. Fix: stop at shoulder height on every rep
- **Both arms simultaneously with heavy weight** — creates excessive lower back stress. Fix: alternate arms or use lighter weight for bilateral raises
- **Rushing the lowering phase** — wastes the eccentric portion. Fix: take 2-3 seconds to lower on every rep
- **Leaning back** — uses the lower back to assist the raise. Fix: keep torso upright and still

## Do You Actually Need Front Raises?

This is an important question. The front deltoid is already heavily stimulated by:

```
Bench Press          → significant front delt activation
Incline Press        → even more front delt activation
Shoulder Press       → primary front delt compound movement
```

Most people who train chest and shoulders regularly do NOT need direct front delt work — the front delt is already the most developed part of the shoulder for most gym-goers.

Front raises are most useful for:
- People who skip pressing movements
- Bodybuilders who need complete front delt detail
- Correcting a specific front delt weakness
- Adding volume at the end of shoulder day

## Where It Fits In A Shoulder Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Band pull aparts and face pulls         (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Shoulder Press                          (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Dumbbell Lateral Raise                  (side delt isolation — do this third)
4. Rear Delt Fly or Face Pull              (rear delt isolation — do this fourth)
5. Front Raise                             (front delt isolation — do this last)
```

Front raises are placed last because the front delt is already pre-fatigued from pressing work. A few focused sets at the end of the session is all that is needed.

## Grip Variations and Their Effects

| Grip | Hand Position | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pronated (palms down) | Palms facing the floor | Maximum front delt activation |
| Neutral (palms in) | Palms facing each other | Comfortable, slightly less isolation |
| Supinated (palms up) | Palms facing ceiling | Less common, more bicep involvement |

## Equipment Variations

| Equipment | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Dumbbells | Most common, easy to control |
| Barbell | Both arms together, heavier loading |
| Cable (low pulley) | Constant tension throughout |
| Weight plate | Unique grip, both hands together |

## Safety Considerations
- Never use heavy weight on front raises — the shoulder joint is in a forward flexed position which can stress the bicep tendon
- If you feel pain at the front of the shoulder or bicep tendon area, stop immediately
- The front delt is a small muscle — it does not need and should not receive heavy loading on isolation work
- Always warm up the shoulder before front raises

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Alternating front raise | One arm at a time | Reduces lower back stress |
| Cable front raise | Low pulley cable | Constant tension |
| Plate front raise | Hold weight plate with both hands | Unique stimulus |
| Incline front raise | Lie on incline bench face down | Greater range of motion |

## Related Exercises
- **Shoulder Press** — primary compound movement for front and overall delt development
- **Dumbbell Lateral Raise** — isolation movement for side delt width
- **Rear Delt Fly** — isolation movement for rear delt development
- **Face Pull** — rear delt and rotator cuff health and balance movement
