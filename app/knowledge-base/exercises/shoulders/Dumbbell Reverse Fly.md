# Dumbbell Reverse Fly

## Overview
The dumbbell reverse fly is an isolation exercise that targets the posterior (rear) deltoid — the back portion of the shoulder that is chronically underdeveloped in most gym-goers. It also works the rhomboids and middle trapezius making it one of the best exercises for upper back and rear shoulder health. Well developed rear delts improve posture, balance out the shoulders from front to back, and give the shoulders a rounder, three-dimensional appearance when viewed from any angle. It is an essential movement in any complete shoulder training program.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Posterior Deltoid (rear delt)
- **Secondary:** Rhomboids, Middle Trapezius
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Rotator Cuff, Infraspinatus

## Equipment
- Two dumbbells (lighter than lateral raises)
- Flat bench (optional for supported variation)

## How To Perform

### Setup — Standing
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart
2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand with palms facing each other
3. Hinge forward at the hips until your torso is roughly parallel to the floor
4. Let the dumbbells hang directly below your chest
5. Maintain a neutral spine — do not round the lower back
6. Keep a slight bend in the elbows throughout

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Raise both arms out to the sides in a wide arc
3. Lead with your elbows — think about driving them toward the ceiling
4. Raise until your arms are parallel to the floor — in line with your shoulders
5. Squeeze the rear delts and rhomboids hard at the top for 1 second
6. Lower the dumbbells slowly back to the starting position — take 3 seconds
7. Do not let the dumbbells swing or touch at the bottom
8. Exhale at the top and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 12-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 3-4 | 15-20 | 30-45 secs |
| Posture and health | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- The reverse fly uses very light weight — do not let ego dictate the load
- Increase weight by 1-2kg only when you can complete all reps with a full range of motion and a strong rear delt squeeze
- The rear delt is a small muscle — it responds better to higher reps and strict form than heavy weight

## Key Cues
- **"Elbows to the ceiling"** — drives rear delt activation instead of trap activation
- **"Squeeze at the top"** — hold the peak contraction for 1 second on every rep
- **"Slow on the way down"** — the 3-second lowering phase is where most muscle growth occurs
- **"Chest parallel to floor"** — maintaining the forward hinge keeps the rear delt as the primary mover
- **"Pinky higher than thumb"** — slight external rotation at the top maximizes rear delt activation

## Common Mistakes

- **Using too much weight** — the most common mistake. Heavy weight causes the traps to take over completely. Fix: use very light dumbbells and focus entirely on feeling the rear delt contract
- **Standing too upright** — reduces rear delt activation significantly. Fix: hinge forward until torso is parallel to the floor
- **Raising too high above shoulder level** — shifts load entirely to the traps. Fix: stop when arms are parallel to the floor
- **Rushing the lowering phase** — removes the most valuable part of the rep. Fix: take 3 full seconds to lower on every rep
- **Rounding the lower back** — places stress on the lumbar spine. Fix: maintain a neutral spine throughout and engage the core

## Standing vs Bench Supported Reverse Fly

| | Standing Reverse Fly | Bench Supported Reverse Fly |
|---|---|---|
| Lower back stress | Moderate | None |
| Core requirement | Higher | Lower |
| Rear delt isolation | High | Higher |
| Cheating risk | Moderate | Very low |
| Best for | General use | Maximum isolation |
| Recommendation | Primary variation | Use when lower back is fatigued |

## Bench Supported Variation
For maximum rear delt isolation with zero lower back involvement:
1. Set a bench to a low incline (15-20 degrees)
2. Lie face down with your chest on the pad
3. Let the dumbbells hang below the bench
4. Perform the same arc movement as the standing variation
5. The bench completely removes the lower back from the equation

## Why Rear Delts Are So Important

Most gym-goers have a severe front-to-back shoulder imbalance:

```
Overtrained (most people):     Undertrained (most people):
→ Anterior deltoid             → Posterior deltoid
→ Chest                        → Upper back
→ Front of body                → Back of body

Result: rounded shoulders, poor posture, increased injury risk
```

Training rear delts directly corrects this imbalance, improves posture, protects the rotator cuff, and gives the shoulder a full three-dimensional appearance rather than looking flat from the side.

## Where It Fits In A Shoulder Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Band pull aparts and face pulls         (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Shoulder Press                          (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Dumbbell Lateral Raise                  (side delt isolation — do this third)
4. Dumbbell Reverse Fly                    (rear delt isolation — do this fourth)
5. Front Raise                             (front delt isolation — do this fifth)
6. Shoulder Shrug                          (trap isolation — do this last)
```

## Safety Considerations
- Always maintain a neutral spine when hinging forward — never round the lower back
- Use very light weight — the rear delt is a small muscle and does not need heavy loading
- If you feel neck or trap pain instead of rear delt activation, reduce the weight further
- Warm up the rear delts and rotator cuff with band pull aparts before this exercise

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Bench supported reverse fly | Face down on incline bench | Maximum isolation, no lower back |
| Cable reverse fly | Cable machine at high pulley | Constant tension throughout |
| Pec deck reverse fly | Reverse pec deck machine | Easy setup, great isolation |
| Single arm reverse fly | One arm at a time | Greater range of motion |
| Seated bent over reverse fly | Seated on bench, hinge forward | Lower back friendly |
