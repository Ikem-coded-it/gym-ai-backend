# Dumbbell Lateral Raise

## Overview
The dumbbell lateral raise is an isolation exercise that targets the lateral (side) deltoid — the muscle responsible for shoulder width and the coveted wide shoulder look. It is the most direct and effective exercise for developing side delt size and is an essential movement in any shoulder training program. While it is a relatively simple movement, most people perform it incorrectly and fail to get the full benefit. Mastering the lateral raise with lighter weight and strict form produces far better results than swinging heavy dumbbells.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Lateral Deltoid (side delt)
- **Secondary:** Anterior Deltoid (front delt), Supraspinatus (rotator cuff)
- **Stabilizers:** Trapezius, Serratus Anterior, Core

## Equipment
- Two dumbbells (lighter than you think you need)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart
2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand at your sides
3. Maintain a slight bend in the elbows throughout — never lock them straight
4. Tilt your torso very slightly forward — about 5-10 degrees
5. Turn your pinkies very slightly upward — as if pouring a glass of water

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core lightly
2. Raise both arms out to the sides in a wide arc
3. Lead with your elbows — not your hands
4. Raise until your arms are parallel to the floor — no higher
5. At the top your elbows should be at shoulder height or very slightly above
6. Hold for 1 second at the top and squeeze the side delts
7. Lower the dumbbells slowly back to the starting position — take 3 seconds
8. Do not let the dumbbells touch your thighs at the bottom — keep tension on the delts
9. Exhale at the top and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-5 | 10-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 3-4 | 15-20 | 30-45 secs |
| Mind-muscle connection | 2-3 | 12-15 | 45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- The lateral raise is not a strength movement — do not chase heavy weights
- Increase dumbbell weight by 1-2kg only when you can complete all reps with strict form and no swinging
- Alternatively increase reps before increasing weight
- Focus on the quality of each rep — feel the side delt working on every single rep

## Key Cues
- **"Lead with the elbows"** — the elbows should be the highest point at the top, not the hands
- **"Pour the water"** — tilt the pinky slightly up at the top for maximum lateral delt activation
- **"Slow on the way down"** — the lowering phase is where most muscle growth happens
- **"Stop at shoulder height"** — going higher recruits the traps, not the delts
- **"Slight forward lean"** — leaning 5-10 degrees forward keeps tension on the lateral delt throughout

## Common Mistakes

- **Using too much weight and swinging** — the most common mistake by far. Swinging uses momentum and completely removes lateral delt activation. Fix: drop the weight significantly and perform strict, controlled reps
- **Raising too high above shoulder level** — shifts load to the traps. Fix: stop when arms are parallel to the floor
- **Thumbs higher than pinkies** — internally rotates the shoulder and reduces lateral delt activation. Fix: tilt pinkies slightly upward
- **Rushing the lowering phase** — wastes the most important part of the rep. Fix: take 3 full seconds to lower the dumbbells on every rep
- **Letting dumbbells touch at the bottom** — releases tension from the lateral delt. Fix: stop just before the dumbbells touch your thighs

## Why Lighter Weight Works Better

The lateral raise is unique — most people use far too much weight:

```
Heavy weight lateral raise:
→ Traps take over
→ Momentum replaces muscle
→ Range of motion reduced
→ Side delt barely works

Light weight strict lateral raise:
→ Side delt does all the work
→ Full range of motion
→ Slow eccentric maximizes growth
→ Side delt fully developed
```

A set of 10kg strict lateral raises produces more side delt growth than a set of 20kg swinging lateral raises. Always.

## Tempo Recommendation
The lateral raise responds exceptionally well to slow tempo:
- **2 seconds** raising (concentric)
- **1 second** hold at the top
- **3 seconds** lowering (eccentric)

This 2-1-3 tempo keeps the lateral delt under tension for the entire set and produces a significantly better pump and growth stimulus than fast reps.

## Where It Fits In A Shoulder Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Band pull aparts and face pulls         (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Shoulder Press                          (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Dumbbell Lateral Raise                  (side delt isolation — do this third)
4. Rear Delt Fly or Face Pull              (rear delt isolation — do this fourth)
5. Front Raise (optional)                  (front delt isolation — do this last)
```

Lateral raises come after the heavy compound pressing work. At this point you can fully focus on isolating the side delt without worrying about fatigue affecting your pressing performance.

## Safety Considerations
- Never raise above shoulder height — the supraspinatus rotator cuff tendon gets impinged at higher angles
- If you feel sharp pain in the top of the shoulder, reduce the range of motion slightly
- The shoulder is a vulnerable joint — always use controlled form and appropriate weight
- Warm up the rotator cuff before lateral raises with band pull aparts or face pulls

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Cable lateral raise | Single cable at low pulley | Constant tension throughout |
| Seated lateral raise | Seated on bench | Removes any leg drive |
| Lean away lateral raise | Hold a rack, lean away | Greater range of motion |
| Partial reps at top | Small pulses at shoulder height | Intense pump at peak contraction |
| Drop set | Reduce weight after failure | Maximum side delt pump |

## Cable vs Dumbbell Lateral Raise

| | Dumbbell Lateral Raise | Cable Lateral Raise |
|---|---|---|
| Tension at bottom | Low | High |
| Tension at top | High | High |
| Resistance curve | Increases toward top | Constant throughout |
| Equipment needed | Dumbbells only | Cable machine |
| Best for | General hypertrophy | Constant tension hypertrophy |
| Use both? | Yes — rotate for variety |

## Related Exercises
- **Shoulder Press** — compound movement for overall shoulder mass
- **Cable Lateral Raise** — constant tension alternative to dumbbell version
- **Face Pull** — rear delt and rotator cuff health movement
- **Rear Delt Fly** — isolation movement for rear deltoid development
