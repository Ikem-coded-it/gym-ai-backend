# Shoulder Press (Overhead Press)

## Overview
The shoulder press, also known as the overhead press (OHP), is the foundational compound movement for building shoulder size, strength, and mass. It is to the shoulders what the bench press is to the chest — the primary heavy compound movement that everything else is built around. It targets all three heads of the deltoid simultaneously while also heavily involving the triceps and upper traps, making it the single most effective shoulder exercise available.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Anterior Deltoid (front), Lateral Deltoid (side), Posterior Deltoid (rear)
- **Secondary:** Triceps, Upper Trapezius
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Rotator Cuff, Serratus Anterior

## Equipment
- Barbell or dumbbells
- Adjustable bench set to 90 degrees (seated) or standing
- Squat rack or shoulder press station

## How To Perform

### Setup (Seated Barbell)
1. Set the bench to 90 degrees and position it inside a squat rack
2. Set the bar at upper chest height on the rack
3. Sit with your back flat against the pad and feet flat on the floor
4. Grip the bar just outside shoulder width — hands slightly wider than your shoulders
5. Unrack the bar and hold it at upper chest level — this is your starting position
6. Elbows should be just slightly in front of the bar — not directly underneath

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core hard
2. Press the bar directly overhead in a straight vertical path
3. At the top the bar should be directly over your ears — not in front of your head
4. Lock out the elbows fully at the top
5. Lower the bar back to upper chest level slowly — take 2-3 seconds
6. Do not let the bar drift forward on the way down
7. Exhale at the top and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 4-6 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 8-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 12-15 | 45-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Add 2.5kg when you can complete all reps across all sets with good form
- The overhead press progresses slower than the bench press — this is normal
- If progress stalls for 2 consecutive sessions, deload by 10% and rebuild

## Key Cues
- **"Bar over ears at the top"** — ensures the bar ends in the correct position directly overhead
- **"Squeeze the glutes"** — prevents lower back hyperextension during heavy sets
- **"Elbows slightly forward"** — keeps the shoulder in a safer pressing position
- **"Full lockout at the top"** — completes the range of motion and maximizes overhead strength
- **"Brace like a plank"** — the core must be maximally tight throughout to protect the lower back

## Common Mistakes

- **Pressing in front of the head instead of overhead** — shifts load away from the shoulders. Fix: press in a straight vertical line directly over the ears
- **Arching the lower back excessively** — compresses the lumbar spine. Fix: squeeze glutes and brace core throughout
- **Flaring elbows too wide** — increases shoulder impingement risk. Fix: keep elbows just slightly in front of the bar
- **Using leg drive on strict press** — turns it into a push press. Fix: keep legs completely still for strict overhead press
- **Not locking out at the top** — reduces the range of motion and limits shoulder development. Fix: fully extend the elbows on every rep

## Seated vs Standing Overhead Press

| | Seated Overhead Press | Standing Overhead Press |
|---|---|---|
| Core involvement | Lower | Higher |
| Weight potential | Slightly higher | Slightly lower |
| Lower back stress | Lower | Higher |
| Balance required | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Shoulder isolation | Full body strength |
| Use both? | Yes — rotate for variety |

## Barbell vs Dumbbell Shoulder Press

| | Barbell Shoulder Press | Dumbbell Shoulder Press |
|---|---|---|
| Weight potential | Higher | Lower |
| Range of motion | Fixed | Greater — more natural |
| Imbalance detection | No | Yes |
| Stability required | Lower | Higher |
| Best for | Strength | Hypertrophy and balance |
| Use both? | Yes — they complement each other |

## Where It Fits In A Shoulder Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Band pull aparts and face pulls         (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Shoulder Press                          (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Lateral Raises                          (side delt isolation — do this third)
4. Rear Delt Fly or Face Pull              (rear delt isolation — do this fourth)
5. Front Raise (optional)                  (front delt isolation — do this last)
```

The shoulder press should always be performed first on shoulder day when your muscles and joints are fresh. It is the most demanding shoulder movement and requires maximum energy and focus.

## Safety Considerations
- Always warm up the rotator cuff thoroughly before heavy overhead pressing
- Never press to absolute failure overhead without a spotter or safety bars
- If you feel shoulder impingement or sharp pain, stop immediately
- The shoulder is a complex ball and socket joint — respect it and never skip the warm up

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 10 arm circles forward and backward
2. 10 band pull aparts
3. 10 face pulls with light resistance
4. 1 x 10 with empty barbell
5. 1 x 6 at 50% of working weight
6. 1 x 4 at 70% of working weight
7. Begin working sets

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Dumbbell shoulder press | Dumbbells instead of barbell | Greater range of motion |
| Arnold press | Rotate from neutral to pronated grip | All three delt heads |
| Push press | Use leg drive to initiate | Heavier loading |
| Z press | Seated on floor, no back support | Maximum core and shoulder demand |
| Machine shoulder press | Fixed path machine | Beginner friendly, lower injury risk |

## Related Exercises
- **Lateral Raise** — isolation movement for side delt width
- **Rear Delt Fly** — isolation movement for rear delt development
- **Face Pull** — rear delt and rotator cuff health movement
- **Upright Row** — compound movement for side delts and traps
