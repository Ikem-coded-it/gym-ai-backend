# Seated Leg Extension

## Overview
The seated leg extension is an isolation exercise that targets the quadriceps exclusively. Unlike compound movements like squats, it works the quads in isolation using a leg extension machine. It is commonly used to finish off the quads after heavy compound work, improve quad definition, and strengthen the knee joint.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Quadriceps (all four heads — rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius)
- **Secondary:** None — this is a pure isolation movement
- **Stabilizers:** Core (lightly)

## Equipment
- Leg extension machine

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Sit on the machine with your back flat against the pad
2. Adjust the seat so the back of your knees align with the edge of the seat
3. Place your ankles behind the lower pad — it should sit just above your ankles, not on your shins
4. Adjust the pad height so your legs are at roughly 90 degrees at the starting position
5. Grip the handles on the sides of the seat for stability

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core lightly
2. Extend your legs upward in a smooth, controlled arc until they are fully straight
3. At the top, squeeze your quads hard for 1 second
4. Lower the weight back down slowly — take 2-3 seconds on the way down
5. Stop just before the weight stack touches down to keep tension on the muscle
6. Repeat for the desired reps

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 3-4 | 6-10 | 60-90 secs |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 10-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 2-3 | 15-25 | 30-45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Increase the weight by the smallest available increment when you can complete all reps with a strong squeeze at the top
- Alternatively, increase reps before increasing weight — go from 12 to 15 reps before adding weight
- Slow down the lowering phase (eccentric) for more muscle stimulus without adding weight

## Key Cues
- **"Squeeze at the top"** — the contraction at full extension is where most quad activation happens
- **"Slow on the way down"** — the lowering phase builds as much muscle as the lifting phase
- **"Don't let the weight crash"** — keep the muscle under tension throughout the entire rep
- **"Toes pulled back (dorsiflexed)"** — pointing toes toward your shins increases quad activation
- **"Stay seated"** — if your hips are lifting off the seat, the weight is too heavy

## Common Mistakes

- **Using too much weight** — causes hips to lift off the seat and reduces quad isolation. Fix: reduce weight and focus on the squeeze
- **Rushing the lowering phase** — wastes the eccentric portion of the rep. Fix: take 2-3 seconds to lower the weight on every rep
- **Partial range of motion** — not reaching full extension misses peak quad contraction. Fix: extend fully and squeeze at the top
- **Incorrect pad placement** — pad on the shin instead of above the ankle puts stress on the wrong part of the leg. Fix: adjust pad to sit just above the ankle
- **Swinging or using momentum** — reduces isolation and increases injury risk. Fix: use a controlled tempo throughout

## Tempo Recommendation
For maximum hypertrophy use a **2-1-3 tempo**:
- **2 seconds** lifting (concentric)
- **1 second** squeeze at the top
- **3 seconds** lowering (eccentric)

## Where It Fits In A Leg Day
The leg extension is an **isolation finisher** — it should come after your heavy compound movements:

```
1. Barbell Back Squat        (heavy compound — do this first)
2. Bulgarian Split Squat     (unilateral compound — do this second)
3. Seated Leg Extension      (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

Doing leg extensions before squats would pre-fatigue your quads and reduce your performance on the more important compound lifts.

## Safety Considerations
- Leg extensions have received criticism for placing stress on the knee ligaments (ACL/PCL) at certain angles — this is more of a concern at very heavy weights
- Keep weights moderate to high rep to minimize joint stress while maximizing muscle stimulus
- If you have existing knee injuries or ACL issues, consult a physiotherapist before including this exercise
- Never lock out the knee aggressively — extend fully but smoothly

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Single leg extension | One leg at a time | Identifies and fixes imbalances |
| Toes pointed out | Externally rotate feet slightly | Emphasizes inner quad (VMO) |
| Toes pointed in | Internally rotate feet slightly | Emphasizes outer quad (vastus lateralis) |
| Drop set | Reduce weight immediately after failure | Maximum pump and hypertrophy |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Back Squat** — primary quad compound movement
- **Bulgarian Split Squat** — unilateral quad and glute compound movement
- **Hack Squat** — machine-based compound quad movement
- **Leg Press** — compound quad movement with lower spinal load
