# Bulgarian Split Squat

## Overview
The Bulgarian Split Squat is a unilateral (single-leg) lower body exercise performed with the rear foot elevated on a bench. It is one of the most effective exercises for building quad size and strength, correcting left-to-right muscle imbalances, and developing single-leg stability. It is significantly more demanding than it looks.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Quadriceps, Glutes
- **Secondary:** Hamstrings, Adductors
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Hip Flexors, Calves

## Equipment
- Dumbbells, barbell, or bodyweight
- Flat bench or elevated surface (45-60cm height)
- Flat-soled shoes or bare feet (recommended)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand approximately 60-90cm in front of a bench
2. Place the top of your rear foot on the bench behind you
3. Your front foot should be far enough forward that your shin stays vertical at the bottom
4. Hold dumbbells at your sides or a barbell across your upper back
5. Stand tall, engage your core, and find your balance before descending

### The Movement
1. Take a deep breath and brace your core
2. Lower your body straight down by bending your front knee
3. Descend until your rear knee is just above or lightly touching the floor
4. Keep your front shin as vertical as possible and your torso upright
5. Drive through your front heel to return to the starting position
6. Complete all reps on one leg before switching
7. Exhale at the top of each rep

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps (per leg) | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 3-4 | 4-6 | 2-3 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 8-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Increase dumbbell weight by 2kg when you can complete all reps on both legs with good form
- Prioritize the weaker leg — always start your sets with it
- If there is a significant strength difference between legs, give the weaker leg an extra set per session until balance is restored

## Key Cues
- **"Straight down, not forward"** — you should descend vertically, not lunge forward
- **"Front heel stays down"** — weight through the heel activates glutes and keeps the knee safe
- **"Tall torso"** — leaning forward shifts load off the quads onto the hip flexors
- **"Squeeze the glute at the top"** — ensures full hip extension and maximum glute activation
- **"Soft landing on the rear knee"** — control the descent, never let the knee crash into the floor

## Common Mistakes

- **Front foot too close to the bench** — causes the knee to travel excessively over the toes and increases joint stress. Fix: move the front foot further forward
- **Leaning too far forward** — reduces quad activation and strains the lower back. Fix: keep chest up and torso vertical
- **Rear knee flaring outward** — indicates hip weakness. Fix: focus on keeping hips square throughout the movement
- **Rushing through reps** — reduces time under tension and increases injury risk. Fix: use a controlled 2-second descent
- **Ignoring the weaker leg** — allows imbalances to grow over time. Fix: always start with the weaker leg and match reps on the stronger side

## Foot Positioning Guide

| Front Foot Position | Effect |
|---|---|
| Further forward | More glute activation, more comfortable for most |
| Closer to bench | More quad activation, harder on the knee |
| Toes straight ahead | More quad focus |
| Slight toe flare (15°) | More comfortable hip position for most people |

## Mobility Requirements
- **Hip flexor flexibility** — tight hip flexors will pull your torso forward and limit depth
- **Ankle dorsiflexion** — needed to keep the front heel flat on the floor
- **Thoracic mobility** — helps maintain an upright torso position

## Safety Tips
- Ensure the bench is stable and will not slide before starting
- Start with bodyweight only to learn the movement pattern before adding load
- If you experience knee pain, check foot positioning and reduce range of motion temporarily
- Do not attempt heavy loads without first mastering bodyweight form

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 10 bodyweight lunges per leg
2. 10 hip flexor stretches per side
3. 1 x 10 bodyweight Bulgarian Split Squats per leg
4. 1 x 8 with light dumbbells
5. Begin working sets

## Comparison To Regular Squats

| | Barbell Back Squat | Bulgarian Split Squat |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Bilateral | Unilateral |
| Spinal Load | High | Low |
| Balance Required | Low | High |
| Imbalance Detection | No | Yes |
| Quad Stretch | Moderate | Deep |
| Difficulty | High | Very High |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Back Squat** — bilateral compound movement, good to pair on the same leg day
- **Romanian Deadlift** — targets the posterior chain, good superset partner
- **Reverse Lunge** — easier unilateral alternative for beginners
- **Step Up** — lower impact unilateral movement for variety
