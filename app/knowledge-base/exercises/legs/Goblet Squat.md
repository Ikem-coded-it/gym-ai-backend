# Goblet Squat

## Overview
The goblet squat is a beginner-friendly compound lower body exercise performed while holding a dumbbell or kettlebell at chest height. It is one of the best movements for reinforcing proper squat mechanics, improving hip and ankle mobility, and warming up the entire lower body before heavier compound work. Despite being considered an entry-level movement, it remains highly effective for muscle development at moderate to high rep ranges.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Quadriceps, Glutes
- **Secondary:** Hamstrings, Adductors, Core
- **Stabilizers:** Upper back, Shoulders (holding the weight)

## Equipment
- Dumbbell or kettlebell

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward (15-30 degrees)
2. Hold a dumbbell vertically at chest height with both hands cupping the top end
3. Or hold a kettlebell by the horns at chest height
4. Keep your elbows pointing downward and your chest tall

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Push your knees outward and sit down between your legs
3. Descend until your elbows touch the inside of your knees or your hips reach below parallel
4. Keep your chest up and your heels flat on the floor throughout
5. At the bottom, use your elbows to push your knees further out for a deeper stretch
6. Drive through your heels to stand back up
7. Squeeze your glutes at the top
8. Exhale and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm up / Mobility | 2-3 | 10-15 | 30-45 secs |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 12-20 | 45-60 secs |
| Technique practice | 2-3 | 8-10 | 60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Increase dumbbell weight by 2-4kg when you can complete all reps with full depth and good form
- Focus on achieving greater depth before adding weight
- At heavier weights the goblet squat transitions from a warm up tool into a genuine strength exercise

## Key Cues
- **"Chest up, elbows down"** — keeps the torso upright and reinforces good squat posture
- **"Sit between your legs, not on top of them"** — encourages proper depth and hip engagement
- **"Knees out"** — push knees outward to track over the toes throughout the movement
- **"Heels on the floor"** — weight should stay through the mid-foot and heel at all times
- **"Elbow to knee at the bottom"** — use the bottom position to actively push knees out and improve mobility

## Common Mistakes

- **Heels rising off the floor** — caused by poor ankle mobility. Fix: elevate heels slightly or work on ankle mobility stretches before squatting
- **Chest collapsing forward** — caused by holding the weight too low. Fix: keep the dumbbell at chest height throughout
- **Not reaching depth** — reduces glute and quad activation. Fix: focus on sitting between the legs and use the elbows to push knees out at the bottom
- **Knees caving inward** — reduces effectiveness and stresses the knee joint. Fix: actively push knees out throughout the entire movement
- **Too light a weight** — removes the counterbalance effect that makes goblet squats effective. Fix: use a weight that challenges you for the target rep range

## Where It Fits In A Leg Day
The goblet squat is best used as a **warm up movement** before heavier compound work:

```
Warm Up:
1. Goblet Squat              (mobility + activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Back Squat        (heavy compound — do this second)
3. Bulgarian Split Squat     (unilateral compound — do this third)
4. Seated Leg Extension      (quad isolation — do this fourth)
5. Seated Hamstring Curl     (hamstring isolation — do this last)
```

The goblet squat is excellent before barbell squats because the counterbalance of the weight naturally teaches you to sit back and down, reinforcing the exact movement pattern you need for heavier squats.

## Why The Goblet Squat Is A Great Warm Up Tool

| Benefit | Why It Helps |
|---|---|
| Reinforces upright torso | Counterbalance teaches chest-up position |
| Opens up the hips | Deep squat position improves hip flexor flexibility |
| Activates the quads and glutes | Primes muscles before heavy compound work |
| Improves ankle mobility | Sitting into the bottom position stretches the ankle |
| Low spinal load | No barbell — safe for warming up |

## Mobility Drill At The Bottom
Use the bottom of the goblet squat as an active mobility drill:
1. Descend to the bottom position
2. Place your elbows on the inside of your knees
3. Push your knees outward with your elbows
4. Hold for 2-3 seconds
5. Stand back up

Doing this on your first 1-2 warm up sets will significantly improve your squat depth and comfort on subsequent barbell sets.

## Safety Considerations
- The goblet squat is one of the safest squat variations due to the low spinal load
- Suitable for all experience levels including beginners and those returning from lower back injuries
- If you experience knee pain, check that your knees are tracking over your toes and not caving inward

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Kettlebell goblet squat | Use kettlebell instead of dumbbell | Slightly different grip, same movement |
| Goblet squat pulse | Small pulses at the bottom | Increases time under tension |
| Tempo goblet squat | 3-second descent | Improves control and mobility |
| Goblet squat with heel elevation | Plates under heels | Increases quad activation and depth |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Back Squat** — primary heavy compound squat movement
- **Bulgarian Split Squat** — unilateral compound leg movement
- **Bodyweight Squat** — even simpler warm up option for beginners
- **Leg Press** — machine-based compound alternative
