# Seated Hamstring Curl

## Overview
The seated hamstring curl is an isolation exercise that targets the hamstrings exclusively using a seated curl machine. Compared to the lying hamstring curl, the seated variation places the hip in a flexed position which puts the hamstrings under a greater stretch, leading to more muscle activation and growth. It is an essential exercise for balanced leg development and knee joint health.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus)
- **Secondary:** Calves (gastrocnemius — lightly)
- **Stabilizers:** Core (lightly)

## Equipment
- Seated hamstring curl machine

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Sit on the machine and adjust the back pad so your knees align with the machine's pivot point
2. Place your legs over the top pad — it should sit just above your ankles
3. Adjust the top pad to sit snugly on top of your lower thighs just above the knee
4. Sit upright with your back flat against the back pad
5. Grip the handles on the sides for stability

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core lightly
2. Curl your legs downward and back in a smooth, controlled arc
3. Pull as far as the machine allows — aim for your heels to come close to the seat
4. Squeeze your hamstrings hard at the bottom for 1 second
5. Slowly return to the starting position — take 2-3 seconds on the way up
6. Stop just before the weight stack touches down to maintain tension
7. Repeat for the desired reps

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 3-4 | 6-10 | 60-90 secs |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 10-15 | 45-60 secs |
| Pump / Finisher | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-45 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Increase weight by the smallest available increment when you can complete all reps with a strong squeeze at the bottom
- Prioritize slowing down the eccentric (return) phase before adding weight
- Use a 2-1-3 tempo for maximum hamstring development

## Key Cues
- **"Squeeze at the bottom"** — peak hamstring contraction happens at full flexion
- **"Slow on the way up"** — the eccentric phase is where most muscle damage and growth occurs
- **"Sit tall"** — an upright torso keeps the hamstrings under a full stretch throughout the movement
- **"Don't let the weight crash"** — maintain tension throughout the entire rep
- **"Pull through the heel"** — focus on driving your heel toward the seat, not just bending the knee

## Common Mistakes

- **Hips lifting off the seat** — reduces hamstring isolation and shifts load to the lower back. Fix: reduce weight and focus on staying seated
- **Rushing the return phase** — wastes the most important part of the rep. Fix: take 2-3 seconds to return to the start on every rep
- **Not reaching full range of motion** — limits hamstring development. Fix: curl as far as the machine allows on every rep
- **Wrong pad placement** — pad too high on the shin puts stress on the wrong area. Fix: adjust pad to sit just above the ankle
- **Using too much weight** — sacrifices form and range of motion. Fix: reduce weight and focus on the squeeze and slow eccentric

## Tempo Recommendation
For maximum hypertrophy use a **2-1-3 tempo**:
- **2 seconds** curling down (concentric)
- **1 second** squeeze at the bottom
- **3 seconds** returning up (eccentric)

## Why Seated Over Lying Hamstring Curl

| | Seated Hamstring Curl | Lying Hamstring Curl |
|---|---|---|
| Hip position | Flexed (stretched) | Extended (shortened) |
| Hamstring stretch | Greater | Lesser |
| Muscle activation | Higher | Lower |
| Comfort | High | Moderate |
| Recommended | Yes — for hypertrophy | Secondary option |

The seated variation is superior for muscle growth because the hip flexion position puts the hamstrings under a greater stretch — a stretched muscle under load produces more growth stimulus.

## Where It Fits In A Leg Day
The seated hamstring curl is an **isolation finisher** for the posterior chain:

```
1. Barbell Back Squat        (heavy compound — do this first)
2. Bulgarian Split Squat     (unilateral compound — do this second)
3. Seated Leg Extension      (quad isolation — do this third)
4. Seated Hamstring Curl     (hamstring isolation — do this last)
```

Training hamstrings after quads ensures your compound movements are not affected by pre-fatigue while still giving the hamstrings dedicated isolation work.

## Safety Considerations
- Never jerk or yank the weight down — always use a smooth, controlled motion
- If you feel a pulling sensation or cramp in the hamstring, reduce the weight immediately
- Ensure the machine pivot point aligns with your knee — misalignment puts stress on the knee joint
- Warm up hamstrings with light sets before going to working weight

## Hamstring Injury Prevention
The hamstrings are one of the most commonly injured muscle groups. To reduce injury risk:
- Never skip hamstring training — quad-to-hamstring strength imbalances increase ACL injury risk
- Include both the seated curl (knee flexion) and Romanian deadlift (hip hinge) for complete hamstring development
- Always warm up before heavy leg work

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Single leg curl | One leg at a time | Identifies and fixes imbalances |
| Drop set | Reduce weight immediately after failure | Maximum pump and hypertrophy |
| Pause rep | Hold at the bottom for 2-3 seconds | Increases time under tension |

## Related Exercises
- **Romanian Deadlift** — hip hinge movement that targets hamstrings through hip extension
- **Barbell Back Squat** — compound movement with secondary hamstring involvement
- **Bulgarian Split Squat** — unilateral movement with hamstring involvement
- **Nordic Hamstring Curl** — advanced bodyweight hamstring exercise for injury prevention
