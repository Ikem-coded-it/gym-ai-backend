# Barbell Back Squat

## Overview
The barbell back squat is a compound lower body exercise and one of the most effective movements for building overall leg strength and size. It primarily targets the quadriceps, glutes, and hamstrings while also engaging the core and lower back as stabilizers.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Quadriceps, Glutes
- **Secondary:** Hamstrings, Adductors
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Erector Spinae, Upper Back

## Equipment
- Barbell
- Squat rack or power rack
- Weight plates
- Flat-soled shoes or squat shoes (recommended)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Set the barbell at upper chest height on the rack
2. Step under the bar and place it across your upper traps (high bar) or rear delts (low bar)
3. Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width
4. Unrack the bar by straightening your legs, take two steps back
5. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward (15-30 degrees)

### The Movement
1. Take a deep breath and brace your core tightly
2. Initiate the movement by pushing your knees out and sitting back and down
3. Descend until your hips are at or below parallel with your knees
4. Keep your chest up and your weight through your mid-foot throughout
5. Drive through your heels to stand back up, pushing your knees out
6. Exhale at the top of the movement
7. Reset your breath and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-6 | 3-5 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-5 | 6-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 15-20 | 30-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Add 2.5kg to the bar each session when you can complete all reps with good form
- If you fail to progress for 2 consecutive sessions, deload by 10% and build back up
- Track every session — weight, sets, and reps

## Key Cues
- **"Chest up"** — prevents rounding of the lower back
- **"Knees out"** — keeps knees tracking over toes, protects joints
- **"Sit between your legs"** — encourages proper depth and hip engagement
- **"Big breath, big brace"** — creates intra-abdominal pressure to protect spine
- **"Drive the floor away"** — cues leg drive on the way up

## Common Mistakes

- **Knees caving inward (valgus collapse)** — weakens the movement and stresses the knee joint. Fix: consciously push knees out throughout the movement
- **Heels rising off the floor** — caused by poor ankle mobility. Fix: elevate heels slightly or work on ankle mobility
- **Forward lean (good morning squat)** — shifts load to lower back. Fix: keep chest up, strengthen upper back
- **Not reaching depth** — reduces glute and hamstring activation. Fix: work on hip and ankle mobility, use a box as a depth guide
- **Looking up too aggressively** — strains the neck. Fix: keep a neutral spine, look slightly downward

## Mobility Requirements
- **Ankle dorsiflexion** — needed to keep heels on the floor at depth
- **Hip flexion** — needed to reach parallel without excessive forward lean
- **Thoracic extension** — needed to keep chest upright

## Safety Tips
- Always use a squat rack with safety bars set just below parallel
- Never squat to failure without a spotter or safety bars in place
- Warm up with bodyweight squats and lighter barbell sets before working weight
- If you feel sharp pain in your knees, hips, or lower back — stop immediately

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 5 minutes light cardio
2. 2 x 10 bodyweight squats
3. 1 x 8 with empty barbell
4. 1 x 5 at 50% of working weight
5. 1 x 3 at 75% of working weight
6. Begin working sets

## Related Exercises
- **Romanian Deadlift** — targets posterior chain, good superset partner
- **Leg Press** — lower back fatigue substitute on high volume days
- **Bulgarian Split Squat** — unilateral variation for addressing imbalances
- **Goblet Squat** — technique builder, great for warming up squat pattern
