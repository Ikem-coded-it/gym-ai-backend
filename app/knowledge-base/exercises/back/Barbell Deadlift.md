# Barbell Deadlift

## Overview
The barbell deadlift is the king of all compound movements. It is the single most effective exercise for building total body strength, posterior chain mass, and overall muscle density. The deadlift works more muscles simultaneously than any other exercise — from the feet to the traps — making it the most efficient movement in any training program. It is one of the three powerlifting movements alongside the squat and bench press and is a true measure of raw strength.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Hamstrings, Glutes, Erector Spinae (lower back)
- **Secondary:** Latissimus Dorsi, Trapezius, Rhomboids
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Forearms, Quads, Calves

## Equipment
- Barbell
- Weight plates
- Flat-soled shoes or deadlift slippers (no raised heels)
- Lifting belt (optional for heavy sets)
- Chalk or lifting straps (optional for grip)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, toes pointing slightly outward
2. Position the bar over the middle of your foot — about 2-3cm from your shins
3. Hinge at the hips and push them back to reach the bar
4. Grip the bar just outside your legs — shoulder width or slightly wider
5. Lower your hips until your shins touch the bar
6. Your shoulder blades should be directly over the bar
7. Flatten your back — neutral spine from head to tailbone
8. Take a big breath into your belly and brace your core as hard as possible
9. Pull the slack out of the bar by engaging the lats before lifting

### The Movement
1. Push the floor away from you — think leg press, not "pull the bar up"
2. Keep the bar dragging up your shins as it rises
3. As the bar passes the knees, drive your hips forward to stand tall
4. Lock out at the top — hips fully extended, glutes squeezed, standing straight
5. Do not hyperextend the lower back at the top
6. Lower the bar by hinging at the hips first, then bending the knees once the bar passes them
7. Either reset completely at the bottom or touch and go for the next rep
8. Exhale at the top and take a fresh breath before each rep

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 1-5 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 6-10 | 2-3 mins |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 10-15 | 60-90 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Add 5kg to the bar each session for beginners — the deadlift progresses fastest of all lifts initially
- Intermediate lifters add 2.5kg per session
- When progress stalls, deload by 10% and rebuild
- Always prioritize form over weight — the deadlift is the exercise most likely to cause serious injury when ego overrides technique

## Key Cues
- **"Push the floor away"** — reframes the deadlift as a push rather than a pull, activating the legs more effectively
- **"Bar stays on the body"** — the bar should drag up the shins and thighs throughout the lift
- **"Chest up, hips down"** — sets the correct starting position every time
- **"Big breath, big brace"** — creates intra-abdominal pressure that protects the spine under heavy load
- **"Squeeze oranges in your armpits"** — activates the lats and keeps the bar close to the body

## Common Mistakes

- **Rounding the lower back** — the most dangerous mistake in all of weight training. Places catastrophic stress on the lumbar discs. Fix: always establish a neutral spine before lifting, reduce weight if back rounds
- **Bar drifting away from the body** — increases the moment arm and makes the lift significantly harder and more dangerous. Fix: keep the bar in contact with your body throughout the entire lift
- **Jerking the bar** — yanking the bar explosively off the floor without taking the slack out first causes a sudden load spike. Fix: pull the slack out slowly, then accelerate
- **Hips shooting up first** — turns the deadlift into a stiff-leg deadlift and overloads the lower back. Fix: push the floor away and keep hips and shoulders rising at the same rate
- **Hyperextending at lockout** — leaning back excessively at the top compresses the lumbar spine. Fix: stand tall and squeeze the glutes — do not lean back

## The Hip Hinge — The Foundation Of The Deadlift
The hip hinge is the most important movement pattern to master before deadlifting heavy:

```
Incorrect:  bending at the waist like touching your toes
            → rounds the spine, dangerous

Correct:    pushing the hips back like closing a car door with your hips
            → maintains neutral spine, safe and powerful
```

Practice the hip hinge with a dowel rod or broomstick against your back before loading the barbell.

## Conventional vs Sumo Deadlift

| | Conventional Deadlift | Sumo Deadlift |
|---|---|---|
| Stance | Hip width | Very wide — outside the arms |
| Grip | Outside the legs | Inside the legs |
| Range of motion | Longer | Shorter |
| Primary target | Hamstrings, lower back | Glutes, quads, inner thigh |
| Best for | Most people | Those with good hip mobility |
| Use both? | Yes — rotate for variety |

## Where It Fits In A Back Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Hip hinges and band pull aparts        (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Barbell Deadlift                       (total body compound — do this second)
3. Pull Ups                               (vertical pull compound — do this third)
4. Barbell Row                            (horizontal pull compound — do this fourth)
5. Lat Pulldown or Cable Row              (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

The deadlift should always be performed first in a back workout — it is the most neurologically demanding exercise in any program and requires maximum energy, focus, and a fresh central nervous system.

## Deadlift vs Squat

```
Squat:     quad dominant    — knee flexion pattern
Deadlift:  hip dominant     — hip hinge pattern

Both are essential — they complement each other perfectly
and together build complete lower body and back development.
```

## Safety Considerations
- Never deadlift with a rounded lower back — this is non-negotiable
- Use a lifting belt on maximum effort sets to support the lower back
- Warm up thoroughly — the deadlift places significant stress on the entire posterior chain
- If you feel any sharp lower back pain, stop immediately and do not continue
- Respect the weight — the deadlift has ended more training careers than any other exercise when performed incorrectly

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 10 bodyweight hip hinges
2. 10 Romanian deadlifts with empty barbell
3. 1 x 5 at 40% of working weight
4. 1 x 4 at 60% of working weight
5. 1 x 2 at 80% of working weight
6. Begin working sets

## Grip Options

| Grip | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Double overhand | Both palms facing you | Beginner and moderate weights |
| Mixed grip | One palm each direction | Heavy weights without straps |
| Hook grip | Thumb under fingers | Powerlifting — very secure |
| Straps | Lifting straps around bar | Maximum weight, removes grip limit |

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift (RDL) | Hinge with soft knees, bar to mid-shin | Hamstring and glute isolation |
| Stiff leg deadlift | Minimal knee bend throughout | Maximum hamstring stretch |
| Sumo deadlift | Wide stance, grip inside legs | Glute and quad emphasis |
| Trap bar deadlift | Hex bar, more upright torso | Back-friendly alternative |
| Deficit deadlift | Stand on plates, greater range | Builds strength off the floor |

## Related Exercises
- **Barbell Back Squat** — the other foundational compound movement
- **Romanian Deadlift** — hamstring isolation variation of the hip hinge
- **Barbell Row** — horizontal pull for back thickness
- **Pull Ups** — vertical pull for lat width and upper back development
