# Pull Ups

## Overview
The pull up is one of the most effective bodyweight compound exercises for building upper back width, thickness, and strength. It targets the latissimus dorsi — the large wing-shaped muscles of the back — along with the biceps and rear deltoids. Pull ups are a true test of relative strength (strength relative to bodyweight) and are considered the upper body equivalent of the squat in terms of overall effectiveness and importance.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Latissimus Dorsi (lats), Biceps Brachii
- **Secondary:** Rhomboids, Rear Deltoids, Teres Major
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Forearms, Trapezius

## Equipment
- Pull up bar (fixed or doorframe)
- Resistance bands (for assisted variation)
- Dip belt with weight plates (for weighted variation)

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Grip the pull up bar slightly wider than shoulder width
2. Use an overhand grip — palms facing away from you
3. Hang at full arm extension — this is your dead hang starting position
4. Engage your core and squeeze your glutes lightly
5. Depress your shoulder blades — pull them down and back before initiating the pull

### The Movement
1. Take a breath and brace your core
2. Initiate the pull by driving your elbows down and back toward your hips
3. Think about pulling your chest to the bar — not your chin over the bar
4. Pull until your chin clears the bar or your upper chest touches it
5. Squeeze the lats and upper back hard at the top
6. Lower yourself slowly back to a full dead hang — take 2-3 seconds
7. Exhale at the top and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 3-6 | 2-3 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 6-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 12-20 | 45-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Begin with assisted pull ups or band-assisted pull ups if you cannot complete full reps
- Once you can complete 3 sets of 12 bodyweight reps, add weight using a dip belt
- Add 2.5kg at a time and build back to 3 sets of 8-10 reps before adding more

## Key Cues
- **"Elbows to hips"** — the single best cue for activating the lats instead of the biceps
- **"Chest to bar"** — encourages full range of motion and proper pulling mechanics
- **"Dead hang at the bottom"** — fully extend the arms at the bottom for maximum lat stretch
- **"Shoulders down before you pull"** — depressing the scapula before pulling protects the shoulder joint
- **"Squeeze the bar"** — improves grip and activates the forearms and upper back

## Common Mistakes

- **Pulling with the biceps instead of the lats** — the most common mistake. The biceps fatigue quickly and limit the lat development. Fix: focus on driving elbows down toward your hips, not pulling with your hands
- **Not reaching full extension at the bottom** — removes the lat stretch and reduces range of motion. Fix: fully extend arms to a dead hang at the bottom of every rep
- **Kipping or swinging** — uses momentum instead of muscle. Fix: perform strict pull ups with no body swing
- **Shrugging the shoulders up** — elevates the scapula and reduces lat activation. Fix: pull the shoulder blades down before initiating every rep
- **Looking straight up** — strains the neck. Fix: keep a neutral spine and look slightly upward naturally

## Grip Variations and Their Effects

| Grip | Hand Position | Primary Target |
|---|---|---|
| Wide overhand | Wider than shoulder width | Lat width |
| Shoulder width overhand | Shoulder width apart | Balanced lat and bicep |
| Neutral grip | Palms facing each other | Biceps and lower lats |
| Underhand (chin up) | Palms facing toward you | Biceps emphasis |

## Pull Up vs Chin Up

| | Pull Up | Chin Up |
|---|---|---|
| Grip | Overhand (pronated) | Underhand (supinated) |
| Primary target | Lats | Biceps and lats |
| Difficulty | Harder | Slightly easier |
| Range of motion | Full | Full |
| Best for | Back width | Bicep and back combination |
| Use both? | Yes — rotate between sessions |

## Where It Fits In A Back Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Dead hangs and scapular pulls          (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Pull Ups                               (vertical pull compound — do this second)
3. Barbell or Dumbbell Row                (horizontal pull compound — do this third)
4. Lat Pulldown                           (vertical pull assistance — do this fourth)
5. Cable Row or Face Pull                 (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

Pull ups should always be performed first on back day when your muscles and grip strength are fresh. They are the most demanding back movement and require maximum energy and focus.

## Building Up To Your First Pull Up
If you cannot yet do a full pull up:

```
Stage 1: Dead hangs          — build grip strength, hang for 3 x 30 seconds
Stage 2: Scapular pulls      — learn scapular depression, 3 x 10
Stage 3: Band assisted       — use a thick resistance band for 3 x 8-10
Stage 4: Negative pull ups   — jump to the top, lower slowly for 3 x 5-8
Stage 5: Full pull ups       — bodyweight, aim for 1 then build
```

## Safety Considerations
- Never pull from a completely cold shoulder — always warm up with dead hangs and arm circles
- If you feel sharp pain in the shoulder or elbow, stop immediately
- Avoid kipping pull ups until you have mastered strict pull ups — kipping places significant stress on the shoulder joint
- Ensure the pull up bar is rated for your bodyweight plus any added weight

## Weighted Progression

| Level | Load |
|---|---|
| Beginner | Bodyweight — master form first |
| Intermediate | +10-20kg via dip belt |
| Advanced | +30kg+ via dip belt |

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Chin up | Underhand grip | More bicep involvement |
| Wide grip pull up | Hands wider than shoulders | Maximum lat width |
| Neutral grip pull up | Palms facing each other | Shoulder-friendly option |
| Weighted pull up | Dip belt with plates | Progressive overload |
| Band assisted pull up | Band under knees | Builds strength for beginners |
| Archer pull up | Extend one arm during pull | Advanced unilateral variation |

## Related Exercises
- **Lat Pulldown** — machine-based vertical pull, great for beginners and high rep work
- **Barbell Row** — horizontal pull compound movement for back thickness
- **Dumbbell Row** — unilateral horizontal pull for back thickness and imbalance correction
- **Face Pull** — rear delt and rotator cuff isolation movement
