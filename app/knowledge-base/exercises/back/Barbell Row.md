# Barbell Row

## Overview
The barbell row is a compound horizontal pulling movement and one of the most effective exercises for building overall back thickness, strength, and mass. It targets the entire back from the lats to the rhomboids, traps, and rear delts simultaneously. Alongside the pull up, it forms the foundation of any serious back training program. It is the horizontal pulling equivalent of the barbell bench press — heavy, effective, and essential.

## Muscles Worked

- **Primary:** Latissimus Dorsi, Rhomboids, Middle Trapezius
- **Secondary:** Rear Deltoids, Biceps Brachii, Lower Trapezius
- **Stabilizers:** Core, Erector Spinae, Hamstrings, Glutes

## Equipment
- Barbell
- Weight plates
- Flat-soled shoes

## How To Perform

### Setup
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed slightly outward
2. Hinge at the hips and push them back until your torso is roughly parallel to the floor
3. Grip the bar just outside shoulder width with an overhand grip
4. The bar should be directly under your shoulder blades
5. Keep your back flat — neutral spine from head to tailbone
6. Engage your core and squeeze your glutes

### The Movement
1. Take a deep breath and brace your core hard
2. Initiate the pull by retracting your shoulder blades first
3. Drive your elbows back and upward toward the ceiling
4. Pull the bar into your lower chest or upper abdomen
5. Squeeze the entire back hard at the top for 1 second
6. Lower the bar slowly back to the starting position — take 2-3 seconds
7. Let the bar hang at full arm extension at the bottom for a full lat stretch
8. Exhale and repeat

## Rep and Set Recommendations

| Goal | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | 4-5 | 4-6 | 3-5 mins |
| Hypertrophy (muscle growth) | 3-4 | 6-12 | 60-90 secs |
| Endurance | 2-3 | 12-15 | 45-60 secs |

## Progressive Overload
- Add 2.5kg to the bar each session when you can complete all reps with good form
- If progress stalls for 2 consecutive sessions, deload by 10% and rebuild
- Track every session — weight, sets, and reps without exception

## Key Cues
- **"Hips back, chest up"** — the two most important setup cues for a safe, effective row
- **"Lead with the elbows"** — drives the back muscles rather than the biceps
- **"Squeeze the shoulder blades together"** — maximizes rhomboid and mid trap activation
- **"Bar to belly button"** — ensures the bar travels to the right contact point
- **"Brace like you're about to get punched"** — creates maximum intra-abdominal pressure to protect the spine

## Common Mistakes

- **Rounding the lower back** — the most dangerous mistake, places severe stress on the lumbar spine. Fix: keep a neutral spine throughout, reduce weight if necessary
- **Jerking or using momentum** — reduces muscle activation and increases injury risk. Fix: use a controlled tempo and reduce weight if needed
- **Standing too upright** — shifts the movement into a shrug rather than a row. Fix: hinge forward until torso is roughly parallel to the floor
- **Pulling to the chest instead of the abdomen** — reduces lat engagement. Fix: pull the bar to your lower chest or upper abdomen
- **Not fully extending at the bottom** — removes the lat stretch. Fix: lower to a full hang at the bottom of every rep

## Grip Options

| Grip | Hand Position | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Overhand (pronated) | Palms facing down | More upper back and rear delt |
| Underhand (supinated) | Palms facing up | More lat and bicep involvement |
| Neutral | Palms facing each other | Requires parallel grip bar |

## Torso Angle Guide

| Torso Angle | Primary Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 45 degrees | Upper back and lats | Easier on lower back |
| Parallel to floor | Full back — lats, rhomboids, traps | Most effective position |
| Below parallel | Lower lats | Very demanding on lower back |

## Where It Fits In A Back Day

```
Warm Up:
1. Dead hangs and band pull aparts        (activation — do this first)

Working Sets:
2. Pull Ups                               (vertical pull compound — do this second)
3. Barbell Row                            (horizontal pull compound — do this third)
4. Lat Pulldown                           (vertical pull assistance — do this fourth)
5. Cable Row or Face Pull                 (isolation finisher — do this last)
```

The barbell row is placed after pull ups because vertical pulling movements generally allow heavier loading and engage the lats more completely. The row then adds thickness and density to the entire back through horizontal pulling.

## Vertical vs Horizontal Pulling

```
Vertical pulls  (pull ups, lat pulldown):
→ Arms travel up and down
→ Builds lat WIDTH — the V-taper look

Horizontal pulls (barbell row, cable row):
→ Arms travel front to back
→ Builds back THICKNESS — the dense, muscular look from the side

You need BOTH for a complete back.
```

## Safety Considerations
- Never sacrifice back position for heavier weight — lower back injuries from rows can be severe and long lasting
- Always warm up the lower back and hamstrings before heavy rows
- If you feel any sharp lower back pain, stop immediately
- Use a lifting belt on maximum effort sets to support the lower back

## Warm Up Protocol
1. 10 hip hinges with bodyweight
2. 10 band pull aparts
3. 1 x 10 with empty barbell
4. 1 x 6 at 50% of working weight
5. 1 x 4 at 70% of working weight
6. Begin working sets

## Variations

| Variation | How | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Pendlay row | Bar returns to floor each rep | Stricter form, more explosive |
| Yates row | More upright torso, underhand grip | More lat and bicep focus |
| Dumbbell row | Single arm with dumbbell | Fixes imbalances, greater range of motion |
| Cable row | Seated cable machine | Constant tension, lower back friendly |
| T-bar row | T-bar machine or landmine | Comfortable grip, heavy loading |

## Related Exercises
- **Pull Ups** — vertical pull compound movement for lat width
- **Dumbbell Row** — unilateral horizontal pull for imbalance correction
- **Lat Pulldown** — machine vertical pull, good for higher rep work
- **Face Pull** — rear delt and rotator cuff isolation and health movement
